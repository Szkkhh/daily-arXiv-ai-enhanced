### [1] [D-Judge: Disrupting Multi-Turn Jailbreaks using Semantics-Preserving Output Rewriting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02640)
*Huanli Gong,Zhipeng Wei,Yu Fu,Haz Sameen Shahgir,Ananya Gupta,Yue Dong,N. Benjamin Erichson*

Main category: cs.CR

TL;DR: D-Judge defends against multi-turn jailbreaks by rewriting model outputs to preserve meaning but distort judge feedback, weakening attack iterations and improving safety.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The work is motivated by a gap in existing defenses: they detect or block unsafe content at individual turns or only at the final answer, but do not break the intermediate judge-driven refinement loop that attackers use to iteratively improve harmful prompts.

Method: D-Judge is a semantics-preserving output rewriting defense against multi-turn jailbreak attacks. It rewrites the victim LLM’s responses before an attacker’s judge model evaluates them, with the goal of misaligning the judge’s feedback signal while preserving the original meaning. The method is trained using a dataset of semantically equivalent response pairs that receive different harmfulness scores, first via supervised fine-tuning and then direct preference optimization.

Result: On HarmBench, D-Judge reduces the success rate of state-of-the-art multi-turn jailbreak attacks and maintains performance on benign benchmarks.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that intervening in the judge-feedback loop via meaning-preserving rewriting can effectively disrupt iterative jailbreak optimization without degrading normal utility.

Abstract: Multi-turn jailbreak attacks pose a growing threat to large language model (LLM) safety because they exploit feedback from auxiliary judge models to iteratively refine prompts toward harmful goals. Existing defenses largely detect or block unsafe content at individual turns or at the final response, leaving the judge-driven refinement loop intact and allowing attackers to extract informative feedback from intermediate interactions. We introduce D-Judge, a semantics-preserving output rewriting defense that intervenes directly in this loop by rewriting the victim LLM's responses before they are evaluated by the attacker's judge. By misaligning the judge's feedback signal without changing the meaning of the original response, D-Judge derails the attacker's prompt-refinement process, causing subsequent queries to be optimized against a distorted signal of attack progress. To improve D-Judge's ability to produce such rewrites, we construct a dataset of semantically equivalent response pairs that induce different judge-assigned harmfulness scores, and use it for supervised fine-tuning followed by direct preference optimization. Experiments on HarmBench show that D-Judge reduces the success rate of state-of-the-art multi-turn jailbreaks while preserving performance on benign benchmarks.
